Research

Our work studies how robots can sense and respond to the people around them. We build interactive robotic systems that integrate sensing, neural and physiological signal processing, and machine learning, and we evaluate them through human-subject experiments.

Current projects at Texas State

Perceived Safety in Human-Robot Collaboration

How people judge whether a collaborative robot is safe to work next to, and how robot motion and behavior shape that judgment, measured through behavioral, physiological, and neural signals.

Brain-Drone Race

Controlling drones with EEG-based brain-computer interfaces, as a platform for studying neural control in real time and for hands-on student learning and outreach.

Drone Design

Design and evaluation of lightweight drones for indoor and emergency-response settings, building on our work on micro-UAVs for first responders.

Past projects

Trust in Human-Robot Interaction for Law Enforcement Communication

This NSF-funded project studied how trust is established during human-robot communication in law enforcement scenarios. We developed a teleoperated communication robot with audio, video, and display interfaces to support remote interaction between officers and the public, and examined how robot design and communication modalities influence trust in public safety contexts.

  • HRI
  • Trust
  • Law enforcement

BCI Exploration of Human Responses to Robot Behaviors

Using brain-computer interface methods, this project studied how people perceive and respond to different robot behaviors during collaborative tasks. EEG and behavioral data were collected while robot expressivity and gestures were varied, to understand how design features influence perceived safety, trust, and engagement.

  • BCI
  • Trust
  • Collaborative robots

Purposeful Communication for Human-Robot Collaboration in Manufacturing

A systematic review of human-robot collaboration systems that identified sources of uncertainty in collaborative tasks and analyzed how communication modalities affect trust and team performance. The project proposed a framework for purposeful human-robot communication to support more reliable and transparent collaboration.

  • Human-robot collaboration
  • Communication
  • Manufacturing

EEG-Based Decoding of Finger Numeral Configurations

This project explored how EEG signals can be used to decode human intention for adaptive interfaces. Machine learning models were developed to classify neural patterns associated with finger numeral configurations and event-related potentials, showing that these neural signatures can be reliably distinguished.

  • EEG
  • Machine learning
  • Neural decoding

Closed-Loop EEG Integration for Rehabilitation Robotics

This project extended EEG-based intention decoding to closed-loop control for rehabilitation. Neural signals were integrated with a functional electrical stimulation (FES) cycling system to modulate cadence in real time based on user intent, showing how brain signals can guide adaptive rehabilitation devices.

  • BCI
  • Rehabilitation
  • Control
