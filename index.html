Human-Centered Robotics and Neurotechnology Lab

We study how robots can understand and adapt to the people they work with. Our lab combines human-robot interaction with neurotechnology, including EEG, brain-computer interfaces, and eye tracking, to measure attention, workload, perceived safety, and trust, and uses those signals to make collaborative robots, drones, and social robots safer and more responsive.

Research areas

Neuroadaptive Human-Robot Collaboration

Using EEG, eye tracking, and other physiological signals to estimate a person’s attention, workload, and trust in real time, so robots can adjust how they move and communicate.

Learn more →

Perceived Safety and Trust in Collaborative Robots

Understanding how people judge whether a robot is safe to work next to, and how robot behavior, motion, and communication shape trust in shared workspaces.

Learn more →

Brain-Computer Interfaces and Human-Drone Interaction

Controlling drones with brain signals and designing lightweight drones for emergency response, with a focus on how people and aerial robots work together.

Principal investigator

Roya Salehzadeh, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor, Ingram School of Engineering

Dr. Salehzadeh’s research focuses on human-robot interaction, brain-computer interfaces, and machine learning for adaptive robotic systems. She received her Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering and M.S. in Computer Science from The University of Alabama, and was previously an Assistant Professor at Lawrence Technological University.
