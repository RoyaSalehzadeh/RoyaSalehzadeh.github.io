I am an Assistant Professor in the Ingram School of Engineering at Texas State University, where I direct the Human-Centered Robotics and Neurotechnology Lab. My research asks how robots can understand and adapt to the people they work with. We combine human-robot interaction with neurotechnology, including EEG, brain-computer interfaces, and eye tracking, to measure attention, workload, perceived safety, and trust, and use those signals to make collaborative robots, drones, and social robots safer and more responsive.

I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering and M.S. in Computer Science from The University of Alabama, where I worked with Prof. Nader Jalili on human-in-the-loop control, trust in human-robot collaboration, and brain-computer interfaces. I also hold an M.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Amirkabir University of Technology and a B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Urmia University. Before joining Texas State, I was an Assistant Professor of Mechanical, Robotics, and Industrial Engineering at Lawrence Technological University.

Prospective PhD students

I am recruiting PhD students to join the Human-Centered Robotics and Neurotechnology Lab. I am looking for students with a background in mechanical, electrical, or computer engineering, computer science, or a related field, and an interest in robotics, brain-computer interfaces, or machine learning. If you are interested, email me at azo29@txstate.edu with your CV, transcripts, and a short note on the research you would like to do, using the subject line “Prospective PhD student.”

Research interests

  • Brain-computer interfaces and neural decoding
  • Perceived safety and trust in collaborative robots
  • Human-drone interaction
  • Machine learning for EEG and physiological signals
  • Learning-based robot decision-making
